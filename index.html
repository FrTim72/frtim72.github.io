I'm Yang. I train foundation models that learn to see, imagine, and act — today humanoid foundation models at Delta Intelligence, and before that video generation foundation models for Vidu at ShengShu.

I was trained in computational mathematics, working on AI × Science at Peking University, then spent years as a senior engineer on industrial-grade communication networks, data, and systems. I like problems where clean math meets messy engineering at scale.

A thread runs through all of it: natural intelligence and systems biology. Studying living systems as dynamics shaped how I think about building machines that perceive and act.

The thread natural intelligence & systems biology

One question runs through my work: how do living systems come to understand and act in the world — and how can machines learn to do the same? I approach intelligence the way a systems biologist approaches a cell: as a stochastic, multiscale, adaptive dynamical system.

  • PKU
    Systems biology as a lensWorking on AI × Science with Prof. Tiejun Li, I learned to model biological systems as stochastic, multiscale dynamics. It shaped how I see intelligence: not a single algorithm, but an adaptive system of many interacting parts.
  • Vidu
    Learning the world by watchingAnimals build an internal model of the world largely from what they see. Training video foundation models is the machine version: learning physics, objects, and causality from pixels.
  • Δ₀
    Natural intelligence as the teacherHumans are the most capable embodied intelligence we know. Δ₀ learns from large-scale human motion data, turning how people move and handle objects into skills for humanoid robots.
